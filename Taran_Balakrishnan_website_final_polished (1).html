The Application of Synthetic Logic Gate Circuits to Treat B-Cell Lymphomas

Parexel · CAGT internal report 2026 Team-reviewed
A working project directive for a multi-antigenic CAR-T therapy — CAR architecture, drug-delivery mechanism, and trial set-up considerations for a Contract Research Organization.

Chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy has transformed outcomes in B-cell malignancies, but single-antigen targeting invites antigen escape and on-target/off-tumour toxicity. This directive proposes a synthetic logic-gate architecture that resolves two antigens in sequence, narrowing the cytotoxic output to tumour cells that present both CD19 and CD20.

Receptor architectures

  • iCAR — inhibitory CAR engages a healthy-cell marker and delivers an ITIM signal, sparing healthy tissue while tumour antigen drives activation.
  • TanCAR — a tandem receptor with two antigen-binding domains; either CD19 or CD20 is sufficient to trigger lysis.
  • SynNotch — sequential AND gate: CD19 binding releases a transcription factor that drives a CD20-specific CAR. Both antigens must be present.

Clinical & CRO considerations

  • Antigen-density thresholds and logic resolution at the tumour margin.
  • Manufacturing workflow for dual-receptor constructs and release assays.
  • Stage 4 trial operations observed for TSHA-102 Rett syndrome gene therapy (Taysha Therapeutics, cleared August 2026).
